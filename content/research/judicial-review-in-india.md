# Judicial Review in India: Scope, Evolution and Constitutional Limitations

## 1. Introduction

Judicial review refers to the constitutional authority of courts to examine the validity of governmental action.

In India, judicial review forms an essential component of constitutional governance. It allows courts to invalidate laws and governmental actions that are inconsistent with constitutional provisions.

The power is particularly important in a written Constitution where governmental institutions derive their authority from constitutional sources.

Judicial review therefore functions as a safeguard against unconstitutional exercises of legislative and executive power.

## 2. Constitutional Foundations

The Constitution does not place judicial review in a single provision. Instead, the power emerges from several constitutional provisions.

Article 13 declares that laws inconsistent with Fundamental Rights are void to the extent of such inconsistency.

Article 32 provides a constitutional remedy before the Supreme Court for enforcement of Fundamental Rights.

Article 226 grants High Courts wide powers to issue writs for enforcement of Fundamental Rights and for other legal purposes.

Articles 136, 141 and 142 also contribute to the constitutional role of the Supreme Court.

Together, these provisions establish a strong constitutional framework for judicial review.

## 3. Judicial Review and Fundamental Rights

Fundamental Rights provide one of the most important fields for judicial review.

Where legislation or executive action violates a Fundamental Right, courts may examine its constitutional validity.

Articles 14, 19 and 21 have particularly generated extensive constitutional jurisprudence.

Judicial interpretation has transformed these provisions from textual guarantees into important safeguards against arbitrary governmental action.

## 4. Evolution of Judicial Review

The development of judicial review in India has occurred through several stages.

Early constitutional decisions focused significantly on the relationship between parliamentary amendment powers and Fundamental Rights.

Over time, the Supreme Court developed broader principles governing constitutional limitations.

The evolution demonstrates that judicial review has moved beyond a narrow examination of legislative competence and has increasingly incorporated principles of fairness, reasonableness and constitutional structure.

## 5. Constitutional Amendments and Judicial Review

The relationship between Parliament's amendment power and judicial review has been central to Indian constitutional jurisprudence.

In *Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala*, the Supreme Court established the Basic Structure Doctrine.

The Court recognised that Parliament may amend the Constitution but cannot destroy its basic structure.

This principle placed substantive constitutional limitations upon the amendment power and preserved judicial review as an essential constitutional feature.

## 6. The Basic Structure Doctrine

The Basic Structure Doctrine is one of the most important limitations on constitutional amendment.

The doctrine does not prevent constitutional change. Instead, it distinguishes between legitimate constitutional amendment and destruction of foundational constitutional principles.

Judicial review, rule of law, constitutional supremacy and democracy have been recognised as important structural principles within the doctrine.

## 7. Judicial Review of Executive Action

Judicial review is not limited to legislation.

Executive and administrative decisions may also be reviewed where they violate constitutional or legal requirements.

Courts may examine issues such as arbitrariness, violation of Fundamental Rights, lack of jurisdiction, procedural unfairness and abuse of statutory power.

However, judicial review generally concerns the legality of decision-making rather than replacing the decision-maker's administrative judgment.

## 8. Judicial Review and Administrative Law

The growth of administrative government has expanded the importance of judicial review.

Modern governments exercise extensive regulatory powers affecting individuals and institutions.

Judicial review provides a mechanism for ensuring that administrative authorities remain within the boundaries established by law.

Principles of natural justice, reasonableness and proportionality have become important aspects of this review.

## 9. Article 32 and Article 226

Article 32 provides direct access to the Supreme Court for enforcement of Fundamental Rights.

Article 226 gives High Courts a wider writ jurisdiction because it extends beyond Fundamental Rights to other legal rights as well.

Both provisions play a significant role in maintaining constitutional accountability.

The availability of constitutional remedies reinforces the practical effectiveness of judicial review.

## 10. Judicial Review and Separation of Powers

Judicial review must operate within the broader principle of separation of powers.

Courts have constitutional responsibility to enforce the law, but they are not intended to function as alternative legislatures or administrative authorities.

Judicial review therefore focuses on constitutional legality rather than political desirability.

This distinction is essential for maintaining institutional balance.

## 11. Judicial Restraint

Judicial restraint is an important complement to judicial review.

Courts may decline to interfere where a matter falls primarily within the policy-making authority of elected institutions and does not involve a clear constitutional violation.

Judicial restraint helps preserve institutional legitimacy and prevents unnecessary constitutional conflict.

## 12. Proportionality and Modern Judicial Review

Modern constitutional adjudication increasingly uses proportionality analysis in appropriate cases involving Fundamental Rights.

The proportionality approach asks whether governmental measures pursue a legitimate objective and whether the restriction imposed upon a right is appropriately connected to that objective.

Its development reflects the changing nature of constitutional review in India.

## 13. Contemporary Significance

Judicial review remains particularly important in areas involving digital privacy, freedom of expression, administrative decision-making, equality and personal liberty.

Technological and social developments create new constitutional questions requiring courts to apply established principles to emerging circumstances.

Judicial review therefore continues to evolve alongside constitutional society.

## 14. Constitutional Limitations

Judicial review itself is subject to constitutional limitations.

Courts must respect legislative competence, constitutional text and institutional boundaries.

The legitimacy of judicial review depends not only upon the willingness of courts to intervene but also upon their ability to exercise that power carefully and consistently.

## 15. Conclusion

Judicial review is an essential component of India's constitutional system.

It protects constitutional supremacy, safeguards Fundamental Rights and ensures that governmental institutions remain within legal and constitutional boundaries.

At the same time, effective judicial review requires judicial discipline and institutional restraint.

The continuing development of doctrines such as the Basic Structure Doctrine, proportionality and constitutional review demonstrates the dynamic role of the judiciary in Indian constitutional law.
